A thought-decoder for the post-EEG era

The bell that rings before you speak.

Earbuds with bone-conduction microphones. A watch with a PPG sensor. A ring with an accelerometer. Together they pick up enough biosignal to recover a small, useful vocabulary of intent — without a training run, because the bridge from signal to meaning is a formal grammar, not a learned weight matrix.

Not another BCI startup.

Brain-computer interfaces promise word-level decoding from invasive electrodes after months of training. Cloister Bell promises something smaller and harder to fake: a dozen distinguishable intents, decoded in real time, from sensors you already wear, with no enrolment phase.

Grammar, not training.

The decoder is built from formal isomorphisms between physiological signal and a small symbolic grammar. It works for any user out of the box because the bridge is mathematical, not statistical. Five bridges ship in v1. The realiser at the end of the pipe is whichever LLM you point it at.
